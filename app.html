      Settings

      Script for names

      The script the names on the clock and in the Today panel are written in (tithi, nakṣatra, yoga, the windows and so on). The dialogs stay in Latin letters.

      Devanāgarī and Kannada are letter for letter. Tamil uses the names of a Tamil pañcāṅga where there is one (Pūrva Phalgunī is பூரம்) and spells the rest by sound.

      Calculation method

      Where the Sun and Moon positions (tithi, nakṣatra, yoga, karaṇa, saṅkrānti, month) come from. Sunrise, moonrise and lagna always use the modern ephemeris.

      The Ayanāṃśa setting is used only for Drik: every other option computes the Sun and Moon by its own text's rules. (Lagna, Jupiter and Rāhu use the Ayanāṃśa under every option.) Expect the Sūrya Siddhānta and Vākya options to differ from Drik by up to a few degrees (hours of tithi time); Ketkar's stays within minutes of it.

      Yogas

      The yogas follow Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi. Daily windows show on the Good / Bad Times ring; tithi-vāra-nakṣatra yogas in the Secondary yogas view; details in Today Details.

      Local practices, shown in the Secondary yogas view only if ticked.

      Ayanāṃśa

      Raman and KP are approximate; true Citrapakṣa isn't implemented.

          How to read the clock

          The clock face is one whole day, from sunrise at the top, clockwise, round to the next sunrise. From the outside in:

          1. Outer ring. One pañcāṅga division at a time: tithi, nakṣatra, muhūrta, yāma, kāla, horā, yoga, karaṇa or lagna. The two arrows at the clock's bottom corners change which one.
          2. Good and bad times. Blue windows are good and grow outward; red windows are bad and grow inward. A cross-hatched part of a red window is one the text relaxes.
          3. The scale. 60 ghaṭīs from sunrise (1 ghaṭī is 24 minutes, 1 pala is 24 seconds). The button in the centre switches it to the 24 clock hours.
          4. Day and night. The amber arc is sunrise to sunset; the black arc is the night.
          5. The hands. The red hand goes round once a day and points at the time now on every ring. The pale hand goes round once a ghaṭī (once an hour in clock-hour mode).
          • White lines mark single moments: Āvartana (midday), Niśītha (midnight), Aruṇodaya (dawn), moonrise and moonset.
          • Faded parts are already over; the part running now has a bright outline.
          • Tap anything on the clock for its name and its start and end, in ghaṭī and in clock time.
          • The Today panel lists the date, the day's tithi, nakṣatra, yoga and karaṇa with when each ends, and the windows running now and coming next.
          • The pencil at the top lets you look at another day or drag the red hand to another time.
